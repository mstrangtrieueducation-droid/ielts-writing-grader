TEACHING, REFERENCE AND REGISTER CONTRACT (teacher clarification 12 October 2026).
Applies only to newly generated homework feedback. Never reinterpret sealed earlier results.

1. Distinguish conventional abbreviations from informal shortening. Established country,
organisation and currency forms (for example the US, the UK, US dollars, the UN, GDP)
are acceptable when their meaning is clear in context. Do NOT require expansion, deduct,
highlight, or invent an optional correction solely because they are shortened forms.
Contractions (don't/can't), texting forms and undefined/ambiguous invented abbreviations
are different categories. Apply the actual register requirement and explain the specific
problem. Check articles, capitalisation, meaning and units independently. US -> United States
alone is not an error correction. This clarification overrides older blanket 'no abbreviations'
rules. Do not turn the examples into a closed whitelist or assume every acronym is acceptable.

2. Before rewriting, map the actually taught requirements to functions in this essay:
overview features and contrast, grouping criterion, comparisons, reference and meaningful
links. Preserve correct student choices. The revision must demonstrate the relevant taught
functions, adapted to these data and the student's sound organisation, not a generic model.
Use learned comparison variants naturally, including numerical openings, while/whereas,
compared with, that/those, with the respective figures being, gaps, equality and ratios
where applicable. Never require every structure, a passive or an unnecessary pronoun merely
to tick boxes. Explain actual unmet learning requirements separately from grammar errors.
Use taught_application_vi to name the original phrase/paragraph and the concrete application
or omission. Recheck that the final revision actually implements that explanation.

3. Resolve each this/that/those/it/they/both/each/respectively/the figure(s)/the opposite
against its antecedent, number, measured quantity, category and group order across sentences.
A clear valid reference is not an error. An unclear but potentially correct reference is
clarity, not proven wrong data. Check references in the revision and suggested sentences too.
Distinguish people participating from proportions and proportions from activities. Numbers
cannot themselves prefer a sport or be attractive to participants. Compare like quantities.

4. Check meaning and collocation of each full phrase, not translated words independently.
Explain the actual semantic mismatch and give natural wording preserving intended meaning.
For example, being well-known does not establish participation frequency; percentages cannot
be attractive to males. Do not accuse a pupil of using translation software or infer intent.
Check every independent error in a clause. Credit valid learned phrases and don't penalise
faithful teacher wording as if the pupil invented it; clarify source imprecision proportionately.

5. A residual chart category such as Other sports is not one named sport. Where the trusted
lesson specifies residual-category-last, describe it AFTER the named categories, at the end
of the final Detail paragraph, and explain a student's misplaced grouping. This is the
teacher's organisation requirement, not a universal IELTS grammar prohibition on 'other'.
Do not apply it to generic 'other countries', to a comparison series 'other workers', or to
unrelated lessons. Never move a phrase without checking the whole paragraph grouping.

6. Before returning the SAME response, compare original -> annotations -> suggestions ->
full revision. No silent substantive repair, no new reference/meaning error in the revision,
no false error introduced to look strict. These checks do not authorise a second paid call.
